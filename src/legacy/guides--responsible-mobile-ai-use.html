Using AI responsibly on your phone

A phone makes it easy to send a screenshot, recording or document in one tap. Check what goes along with it before you share.

Prepare a smaller, safer input

For a translation or summary, copy only the passage you need. Replace names, account numbers and addresses with placeholders. Crop screenshots to the section you want reviewed, then reopen the saved copy and zoom in to check what remains visible. Look for notifications, QR codes, faces and names along the edges.

Before sending a voice clip, check that it includes only the people who agreed to share it. A short typed extract may do the job when a recording also contains private conversation.

Review permissions and data handling

On Android, open Settings, select the app and review its permissions. Exact labels and options vary by device. Allow microphone access when you use voice, and share individual photos through the system photo picker when that option is available. Review the publisher’s privacy information for how prompts, attachments and chat history are stored, sent or deleted.

If the app does not explain whether a feature sends an attachment to a server, use a fictional example or a short redacted passage while you check.

Check the result before forwarding it

For an event message, verify the date, time zone, address and contact details against the original notice. For a translation, compare the names, numbers and meaning against the original with a fluent reader. Check screenshots of the response for account details or other parts of the conversation before posting them.

If you accidentally share a password or access code, change or revoke it immediately. Delete the conversation where supported and use the service’s privacy controls or support channel to understand what deletion covers.
