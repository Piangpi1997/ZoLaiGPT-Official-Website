How to write better prompts for AI chat

A good prompt says what you want, who it is for, and what a good answer looks like. Learn the habits that make AI answers useful.

How to use this guide: This educational text was prepared with AI assistance and reviewed against the linked reference materials. It explains prompt-writing habits for any AI chat app. It is not a software security certification or a substitute for checking the current official instructions. Report corrections by email.

A prompt is a request, not a magic spell

A prompt is the message you type to an AI chat app. The AI does not read your mind — it works only from the words you give it. Two people can ask about the same topic and get very different answers, because the prompt, not the topic, decides what comes back. Learning to write a good prompt is mostly learning to be clear about three things: what you want, what it is for, and what a good answer looks like.

This guide works for any AI chat app. It is not about secret tricks or "magic words". The techniques below are ordinary, repeatable habits: be specific, give context, ask for a format, do one job at a time, and talk back when the answer is wrong.

Say what you want, not just the topic

The most common weak prompt names a topic and stops there. "Teach me English" could mean grammar, vocabulary, pronunciation or exam preparation — the AI has to guess, and it will usually guess the most generic option. A strong prompt names the task, the level and the output:

Weak:  Teach me English.

Strong: I am a beginner learning English. Teach me 10 everyday
greetings, with one short example sentence for each. Use
simple words and keep each explanation under two lines.

Notice what the strong version adds: who you are (a beginner), how many items you want (10), what kind (everyday greetings), what each item should contain (an example sentence), and the style (simple words, short explanations). None of that is advanced technique — it is just saying what you actually want.

The same habit works in Myanmar. Compare a vague request with a specific one:

အားနည်းသော မေးခွန်း: အင်္ဂလိပ်စာ သင်ပေးပါ။

ပိုကောင်းသော မေးခွန်း: ကျွန်တော် အင်္ဂလိပ်စာ အခြေခံအဆင့်ပါ။
နေ့စဉ်သုံး နှုတ်ဆက်စကား ၁၀ ခု သင်ပေးပါ။ တစ်ခုချင်းစီမှာ
ဥပမာဝါကျတို တစ်ကြောင်းစီ ထည့်ပေးပါ။

Give the AI context it cannot guess

An AI chat app does not know your situation unless you tell it. "Write a message to my teacher" is a different job depending on whether the message is an apology, a thank-you note or a request for extra time. Add the facts that change the answer: who the message is for, what happened, what tone you need, and any limits such as length.

Useful context usually fits in one or two sentences. "I am writing to my English teacher to ask for one more day to finish my homework because I was sick yesterday. Keep it polite and under 80 words." That is enough. You do not need to write your life story — just the facts that decide the shape of the answer.

Ask for the format you want

If you do not ask for a format, the AI will choose one for you, and it may not be the one you need. Asking for a format is free and it usually improves the answer a lot. Try phrases like these:

  • "Answer in a numbered list."
  • "Give me a table with two columns: word and example sentence."
  • "Explain it in three short paragraphs."
  • "First give the short answer, then explain why."
  • "Write it as a dialogue between a shopkeeper and a customer."

Format requests also make it easier to spot mistakes. A table with clear columns is easier to check than a long block of text, and a numbered list makes it obvious when a step is missing.

One job at a time

Long prompts that ask for five different things usually get a worse answer than five short prompts. The AI tries to cover everything at once and ends up doing each part thinly. Split the work: first ask for the draft, then ask for corrections, then ask for a shorter version. Each step is a separate prompt, and each answer is easier to judge.

A good rule is one verb per prompt. "Write a paragraph" is one job. "Write a paragraph, translate it, check the grammar and make it funny" is four jobs. Do them in order and the result will be better every time.

Talk back: iterate and correct

A first answer is a draft, not a final result. If the answer is too long, say "make it shorter". If it uses words you do not know, say "explain it with simpler words". If it is wrong, say what is wrong and ask again. Good prompting is a conversation, not a single perfect sentence.

It also helps to tell the AI what to keep. "Keep the first three examples, but replace the last two with examples about food" is a precise correction. Vague complaints such as "that is bad" give the AI nothing to work with.

Ask it to show its work

For questions with steps — maths, grammar, planning — ask the AI to show each step instead of jumping to the answer. "Show each step" or "explain your reasoning in two or three sentences" makes errors visible. If a step is wrong, you can point at that step instead of arguing with the final answer. This is especially useful when you are learning: the steps are often more valuable than the answer itself.

What not to put in a prompt

Some things should never go into an AI chat, no matter how helpful the answer might be:

  • Passwords, PINs, one-time codes or private API keys.
  • Identity numbers, bank details or photos of documents.
  • Your home address, phone number or other people's personal details.
  • Confidential work documents or private messages from someone else.

Chat text can be stored, reviewed or processed on servers outside your phone. If a task needs sensitive information, do it yourself or with a person you trust — not with a chatbot. The safety guide on this site explains this in more detail.

Practice on the website chat demo

The chat on zolaigptofficial.com is a local scripted demo with no live AI connection. It is useful for seeing how a chat interface works and for practising how to type a clear, specific prompt — but its replies are pre-written, so it will not test your prompting the way a live AI chat app does. Treat the demo as a rehearsal space: write your prompt there, check that it is specific and complete, and then use the same habit in a real AI chat app.

Remember: A good prompt says what you want, who it is for, and what a good answer looks like. If the answer is wrong, the fix is usually a better prompt — not a better AI.
