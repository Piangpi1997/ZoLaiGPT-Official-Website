Staying safe when you chat with AI

Treat every chat as a written record. Learn what to keep out of AI chats — and what to do if a mistake has already happened.

How to use this guide: This educational text was prepared with AI assistance and reviewed against the linked reference materials. It explains AI chat privacy and safety habits. It is not legal or security advice, or a substitute for checking the current official instructions. Report corrections by email.

Treat every chat as a written record

When you type a message into an AI chat app, assume it is written down somewhere. Many AI services store conversations on their servers, use them to improve their products, and in some cases have human reviewers look at samples. That does not mean you should be afraid to use AI chat — it means you should decide what you are willing to put in writing before you type it.

A useful habit is the "stranger test": before you send a message, ask yourself whether you would hand the same text, printed on paper, to a stranger. If the answer is no, do not send it. This one habit prevents most privacy mistakes with AI chat.

Seven things never to paste into an AI chat

  1. Passwords, PINs and one-time codes. Never ask an AI to remember, check or improve a password. If you need a strong password, use a password manager or your device's built-in generator.
  2. Private API keys or access tokens. A key pasted into a chat can be stored in logs and is very hard to take back. If a key was ever pasted, treat it as exposed and replace it.
  3. Identity and bank details. National ID numbers, passport numbers, bank account numbers and card numbers have no place in a chat with an AI.
  4. Photos of documents. Do not upload photos of your ID, passport, bills or certificates to "check" them. The text in the photo can be read and stored.
  5. Your home address or other people's personal details. This includes family members' phone numbers, addresses and workplaces. Other people did not agree to share their information.
  6. Confidential work material. Company documents, customer lists, exam papers and unreleased project files should not be pasted into a chat app, especially a free one.
  7. Private messages about someone else. Do not paste someone's private messages into an AI chat to ask what they "really mean". That breaks their trust as well as their privacy.

Free AI tools and your data

ZoLaiGPT is a free project, and many AI tools are free to use. Free does not automatically mean unsafe, but it is worth understanding the trade: a free service still has servers, staff and costs, and your messages may be used to operate or improve that service. Read the privacy policy of any AI app before you use it for anything personal, and prefer tools that explain in plain language what they store and for how long. If a tool's privacy policy is missing or unreadable, treat that as a warning sign.

Also remember that "free" and "private" are different promises. An app can be free and still store your chats; an app can be paid and still store your chats. The price does not tell you the privacy policy — the written policy does.

Children and AI chat

Children should not chat with AI apps alone. An AI can sound confident while giving wrong answers, and it cannot tell the difference between a homework question and a request for personal information. If a child in your family uses an AI chat app, sit with them the first few times, explain the "never share" list above in simple words, and check the chat history together. Teach them the same rule adults should follow: never tell a chatbot your name, school, address or passwords, and never believe everything it says.

Scams, impersonation and fake helpers

AI chat is also used by scammers. Be suspicious of any message — inside or outside a chat app — that claims to be from a company, a bank, a government office or a friend in trouble, and asks you to send money, share a code or click a link. An AI can write a convincing message in perfect English or Myanmar, so good grammar is no longer proof that a message is genuine.

Three quick checks help: first, contact the person or organisation through a channel you already trust, not through the link in the suspicious message. Second, never share a one-time code with anyone, for any reason — real support staff never ask for one. Third, if a message creates urgency ("act now or your account closes"), slow down: urgency is the scammer's favourite tool.

Check important answers before you act

AI chat apps can give wrong answers about health, law, money and safety — and they can state those answers with total confidence. Never act on AI advice alone for anything that affects your health, your legal rights or your money. For health questions, talk to a qualified health worker. For legal questions, talk to a lawyer or a legal aid service. For money questions, talk to your bank or a licensed adviser. Use the AI answer as a starting point for questions to ask a real expert, not as the decision itself.

If you already shared something sensitive

Mistakes happen. If you realise you pasted something sensitive into an AI chat, act quickly and calmly:

  1. Passwords: change the password immediately, and change it anywhere else you used the same one.
  2. API keys or tokens: revoke the key at the service that issued it and generate a new one.
  3. Bank or card details: contact your bank and ask what to do; they may block the card or watch the account.
  4. Identity documents: keep a note of what was shared and when, and watch for unusual account activity.
  5. Other people's information: tell the person involved, so they can take their own precautions.

Deleting the message in your chat history may remove it from your screen, but it does not guarantee it was deleted from the service's servers. That is why the steps above matter more than the delete button.

Staying safe on this website

The chat on zolaigptofficial.com is a local scripted demo with no live AI connection, so the words you type there are not sent to an AI service. That makes it a safe place to practise. Even so, keep the same habits everywhere: do not type passwords, codes or personal details into any chat box, demo or not. Good habits only protect you if you use them every time.
